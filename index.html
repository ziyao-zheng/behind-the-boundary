The central question

Who protects the people responsible for protecting others?

The work is visible. Its costs are not always seen. Behind the Boundary examines what institutions ask of workers—and what they accept responsibility for in return.

Across arts, media, archives, sports, and related professions, this independent study investigates psychological harm, harassment, traumatic exposure, and the limits of institutional care.

Where does the boundary fall?

Between the responsibilities organizations expect workers to fulfil and the responsibilities they accept for protecting them. This project asks how that boundary is defined, experienced, and challenged.

Look beneath the public-facing role

A profession's visible output may reveal little about the exposure, emotional responsibility, or support involved in producing it. The case studies provide a framework for investigating those questions; verified case-specific evidence is still to be added.

Reading the evidence

A commitment is a starting point for inquiry. Organizational statements, documented worker experiences, and evidence limitations are presented separately. Missing evidence does not establish that protections are absent.

Reflection

Beyond the Boundary:
Rethinking Institutional Care

What might meaningful workplace protection involve? Use these questions to reflect on the difference between a formal commitment and care that people can access.

These prompts support critical inquiry. They are not a validated assessment tool or an organizational rating.

Recognition: Does an organization formally recognize psychological harm as an occupational risk?

Consider what a policy explicitly recognizes, whose work it covers, and how recognition translates into practice.

Access: Can workers realistically access the protections being offered?

Consider eligibility, cost, confidentiality, time, and the practical barriers workers may face.

Prevention: Are protections preventive or primarily reactive?

Ask what happens before exposure or harm, as well as what support follows it.

Responsibility: Who is responsible for protecting freelancers, contractors, and temporary workers?

Consider how employment arrangements affect eligibility and where responsibility may remain unclear.

Effectiveness: How can institutions demonstrate that their protective measures are effective?

Ask what evidence would show meaningful change, how workers participate, and what remains unknown.

Keep the question open.

Protection can be examined through recognition, access, prevention, responsibility, and effectiveness. The purpose is to ask what the evidence supports—and where further inquiry is needed.

About the project

About the research.

Behind the Boundary is a university independent study in arts management, created for students and emerging professionals in arts administration and organizational management.

Purpose & approach

The project explores psychological harm, harassment, traumatic exposure, and the relationship between institutional commitments and meaningful workplace protections. It also invites engagement from unions, professional associations, and worker advocates.

A shared set of questions makes comparison possible without treating different professions, employment relationships, or experiences as equivalent.

Two approaches to a case study

Incident-based research

Examines a specific event or set of events, with attention to chronology, source attribution, and the distinction between reported allegations and established facts.

Occupation-based research

Examines recurring occupational risks through academic research and other verified sources. Findings must be read within their study populations, methods, and limitations.

The approach for each individual case will be identified when verified research is added.

Evidence & limitations

This is a developing research framework. The current case pages and comparison panels contain placeholders. No organizational policies, testimonies, statistics, or research findings have been supplied.

Future entries should identify established facts, reported allegations, institutional claims, and research interpretations explicitly, with citations. Source availability, reporting practices, and differences between occupations may limit comparison. A lack of evidence does not establish that protections are absent.

Sources & references

Academic references, news reports, institutional statements, and other sources will be recorded with links and notes about their role and limitations.
